Designing a Marathon Timing Solution on AWS – An Architect’s Perspective

Posted by Saravanesh on September 1, 2025

Last month, I ran my second marathon of the year — and this time I finished with a better result than my first attempt (not really!). While I was catching my breath every ten minutes, a thought kept running: how do these events actually track thousands of runners in real-time? It’s clearly not just someone with a stopwatch and clipboard at the end of the race. Behind the scenes, there’s a complex system ensuring every runner’s time is captured and displayed accurately.

Now, let’s put on our architect hats. (Not the fancy construction helmets — though sometimes it feels like we’re building skyscrapers out of APIs and VPCs). As architects, our job isn’t just to pick services and draw pretty diagrams; it’s to design systems that are reliable, secure, cost-effective, high-performing, and operationally sound. In other words: the AWS Well-Architected Framework is our finish line guide.

In this blog, I’ll cover two things, from an architect’s point of view:

  1. The 5 important things to consider when designing a marathon timing solution in AWS (based on the pillars).
  2. The 5 architect-level steps to apply them — not coding or building, but defining, documenting, governing, and evolving the design.

Think of it as planning a marathon — not running it. We’ll focus on strategy, pacing, and ensuring the race can even happen in the first place.

Project Deliverables:

  • Real-time runner tracking using IoT tags (RFID chips / smartwatches).
  • Accurate timing data stored in DynamoDB with millisecond precision.
  • Live leaderboards hosted on S3 + CloudFront, accessible via web and mobile.
  • Alerts and notifications for race organizers if data flow is interrupted.
  • Operational monitoring & dashboards to ensure reliability and performance during race day.
  • Secure and scalable AWS foundation that can handle thousands of participants with minimal latency.

1. The 5 Important Things to Consider

Operational Excellence
  • Clear monitoring for timing data flow (CloudWatch, X-Ray).
  • Dashboards for real-time runner tracking.
  • Automated deployments for updates so nothing breaks on race day.
Security
  • Encrypt runner data (KMS, TLS).
  • Strict IAM roles and least-privilege policies.
  • VPC isolation and Security Groups to protect the system.
Reliability
  • Multi-AZ for fault tolerance.
  • Auto Scaling for peak loads (everyone finishes around the same time).
  • Backup and failover for critical databases.
Performance Efficiency
  • DynamoDB for fast timing writes.
  • ElastiCache to speed up leaderboards.
  • CloudFront to deliver global leaderboard pages quickly.
Cost Optimization
  • Serverless (Lambda, API Gateway) for burst traffic.
  • Lifecycle policies for S3 to store history cheaply.
  • Spot instances for background analytics.

2. The 5 Architect-Level Steps to Apply Them

Step 1: Define Requirements & Priorities
  • Business goal: Accurate, real-time results.
  • NFRs: Latency < 2 seconds, RPO near zero, 99.9% uptime.
  • Priority: Reliability + Performance (nobody forgives slow or missing results).
Step 2: Document the Architecture (HLD & LLD)
  • Create a High-Level Design (HLD) showing data flows, services, and interactions.
  • Drill into Low-Level Design (LLD) for components like DynamoDB schema, IAM roles, and Lambda triggers.
  • Present these designs to stakeholders for review and refinement.
  • Keep documentation as a living artifact, evolving with the system.

Step 3: Govern & Guide Implementation
  • Set standards for tagging, logging, and security.
  • Define IaC approach (CloudFormation, Terraform).
  • Act as reviewer and guide for dev/ops teams during build.
Step 4: Validate Against Well-Architected Pillars
  • Well-Architected Tool reviews.
  • Security audits with GuardDuty, Security Hub.
  • Reliability tests (chaos testing).
  • Cost checks in Cost Explorer.
Step 5: Continuous Review & Evolution
  • Regular architecture reviews.
  • Optimize compute (move to Graviton).
  • Scale caching strategies as marathons grow.

Conclusion

A marathon timing system isn’t just about tracking who runs fastest — it’s about designing a system that never misses a beat, even under pressure. As architects, we don’t run the race ourselves; we design the track, ensure it’s safe, and make sure the finish line is always there.

The AWS Well-Architected Framework gives us the perfect way to ensure our design is not just functional, but battle-tested for scale, security, and efficiency. Because in cloud architecture — much like in marathons — it’s not just about the sprint, it’s about finishing strong.
